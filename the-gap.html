01 The Awareness-to-Action Gap

Caring is common. Acting is rare. Why?

Most people in the UK say they're worried about climate change [VERIFY SOURCE]. Far fewer have ever been to a talk, joined a group or volunteered [VERIFY SOURCE]. The space between caring and doing is what we call the Awareness-to-Action Gap.

It's not that people don't care.

The gap isn't about laziness or ignorance. Most of us already know the problem is real. What's missing is an easy, believable way in: something close to home, affordable, and not scary to try.

That's what this site is for. We collect friendly London events, tell you honestly what they're like, and help you pick a first one.

Knowing isn't the hard part. Starting is.

02 Why it happens

Five things that get in the way

  1. 01

    A finite pool of worries

    We only have so much room to worry. When rent, bills and food cost more, climate gets pushed down the list. That's not apathy. It's being human.

    What helpsFree, local things that fit around real life.

  2. 02

    Too big, too far away

    Melting ice caps and global targets are hard to picture, and even harder to feel you could change.

    What helpsSomething you can see and touch: a fixed lamp, a cleaner river, a garden.

  3. 03

    Somewhere new, maybe alone

    Not knowing what to expect, who'll be there, or whether you'll fit in is often enough to stay at home.

    What helpsKnowing what it's like first. That's why we go ourselves and write it up in We went.

  4. 04

    Guilt pushes people away

    “Buy an electric car, fit a heat pump” can feel like a club you can't afford to join. Being made to feel bad makes people switch off, not step up.

    What helpsActions that cost little or nothing, offered as an invitation, not a telling-off.

  5. 05

    Despair, or blind optimism

    “It's too late” and “someone will sort it out” lead to the same place: doing nothing. What moves people is constructive hope, the feeling that you can be part of the solution.

    What helpsSeeing ordinary people doing something, and joining in.

03 How to close it

A ladder, not a leap

Each step is a little bigger than the last. Start on any rung. Coming back is the most important part.

  1. Step 01 Learn Go to a talk

    Listen, ask questions, and notice you're not the only one who cares.

    Find a talk →
  2. Step 02 Try Join a workshop

    Fix, make or grow something. Small, hands-on, and low pressure.

    Find a workshop →
  3. Step 03 Do Volunteer for a day

    Restore a river, tend a garden. See the change with your own eyes.

    Find volunteering →
  4. Step 04 Join Make it a habit

    Find your people and go back. Regular sessions turn one step into many.

04 Norms change fast

What's normal can flip.

Not long ago, smoking in pubs was completely normal. After the ban in England in 2007 [VERIFY SOURCE], it quickly became hard to imagine.

Habits spread through people we know. Research suggests that once roughly a quarter of a group takes up a new behaviour, it can tip for everyone else [VERIFY SOURCE].

05 Together beats alone

You don't have to do it all.

Acting with other people is easier to start, easier to keep going, and adds up to more than acting alone. It's also a lot more fun.

You don't need to fix everything. Just do one thing, with someone.
